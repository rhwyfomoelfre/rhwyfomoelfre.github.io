<div class="page-hero"><div class="shell"><div class="eyebrow">Technique</div><h1>Stroke guide</h1></div></div>
<div class="shell content-shell"><article class="prose"><p><strong><em>This article is from the Lyme Regis Gig Club. Although it applies to gig rowing, the technique is very similar to our technique and is a useful reference for coastal rowing.</em></strong></p><h2>Posture</h2><ul><li>Place the oar between the pins with the midpoint of the leather between them.</li><li>Keep the inside hand on top of the handle and the outside hand underneath. Hands should be about one to one-and-a-half hand widths apart.</li><li>Keep shoulders back and down, core muscles engaged, arms straight and legs bent close to 45 degrees.</li><li>Place the front of the feet firmly on the stretcher, with the inside foot near the centre post.</li></ul><h2>The catch</h2><ul><li>Rock over the hips while keeping the back straight and shoulders relaxed.</li><li>Lift the hands from the shoulder and drop the blade square into the water so it is just covered.</li><li>Drive from flexed to straight legs. There should be a distinct feeling of hanging off the oar as the legs drive.</li></ul><h2>The drive</h2><p><strong>The blade must accelerate through the stroke from catch to finish.</strong></p><p>Drive with the legs first, then add the back and shoulders, and finally the arms. The back and arms connect the leg drive to the oar handle; they should not absorb the power. The blade should remain at the same depth and the oar should be pulled around the pin in an arc.</p><h2>The kinetic chain</h2><p>Maximum power comes from sequencing the strongest muscles to the weakest: <strong>legs, then back and shoulders, finally arms</strong>. Keep the trunk square to the boat and rotate through the shoulders.</p><h2>The finish and recovery</h2><p>Extract the blade square with a light tap down from the inside hand. The recovery reverses the drive: arms straighten, body rocks forward, then legs bend. “Hands away” lets the boat run after the power input and prevents the recovery from wasting speed.</p><h2>Common faults</h2><ul><li>Poor posture or hunched shoulders.</li><li>Pulling before the catch or hovering at the catch.</li><li>Arms breaking too early.</li><li>Pulling up on the oar instead of rowing the boat past the blade.</li><li>A rough recovery that disturbs the boat's run.</li><li>Blade too deep, catching crabs, or a short stroke caused by poor trunk flexibility.</li></ul></article><aside class="sidebar"><h2>Training</h2><a href="{{ '/training/' | relative_url }}">Training overview</a><a href="{{ '/boats/' | relative_url }}">Our boats</a><a href="{{ '/join/' | relative_url }}">Try a session</a></aside></div>
